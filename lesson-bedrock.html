Lesson 4 — Amazon Bedrock (the exam’s home planet)

If the stem says foundation model, chatbot, RAG, agents, or Guardrails and “no servers / no ML team,” the answer is almost always a Bedrock feature.

What Bedrock is

A fully managed API that lets you call foundation models from several providers through one interface. You do not manage GPUs. You request model access, then invoke.

Providers to recognise: Amazon (Nova, Titan), Anthropic (Claude), Meta (Llama), Cohere, Mistral, AI21, Stability AI. Titan Image watermarking still appears in older notes — Amazon marks generated images for responsible-AI tracing.

Exam fact: Bedrock does not use your prompts or outputs to train the base provider models. Data stays in your account/region by default.

Bedrock versus SageMaker — say it until it is boring

Bedrock: consume FMs. Lowest barrier. Knowledge Bases, Guardrails, Agents included.
SageMaker AI: you own training data science, instances, endpoints, custom algorithms, notebooks, pipelines.

JumpStart sits on the SageMaker side when you want a one-click open-source or custom weight that is not (only) a Bedrock catalog model.

From your AIP notes — practitioner trim

  • Prefer Knowledge Bases over building OpenSearch + embeddings yourself unless the stem forces custom control.
  • On-demand = token bill. Provisioned = capacity bill. Streaming helps perceived latency, not always cost.
  • Cross-region inference can leave the original region. Residency questions care about that.
  • Do not pick “PartyRock” as a production architecture. It was a playground; the 2026 guide names Bedrock, JumpStart, Q, Quick, Kiro, Strands, AgentCore.
